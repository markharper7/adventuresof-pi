# Methods for Estimating Pi

## Geometric / classical
1. Archimedes' method — inscribed/circumscribed polygons with increasing sides bounding a circle.
2. Measuring a circle's circumference/diameter directly (C/d).

## Infinite series
3. Leibniz series: π/4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + ...
4. Nilakantha series: π = 3 + 4/(2·3·4) − 4/(4·5·6) + ...
5. Machin-like formulas: π/4 = 4·arctan(1/5) − arctan(1/239), using Taylor series for arctan.
6. Ramanujan's rapidly converging series.
7. Chudnovsky algorithm (used in record-breaking computations, basis of `y-cruncher`).
8. Basel problem connection: π²/6 = Σ 1/n², so π = √(6·Σ1/n²).

## Monte Carlo / probabilistic
9. Random point sampling in a square containing a quarter circle — ratio of points inside the circle estimates π/4.

10. Buffon's needle problem — dropping needles on lined paper, using the probability of crossing a line.

## Continued fractions / products
11. Wallis product: π/2 = (2/1)(2/3)(4/3)(4/5)(6/5)(6/7)...
12. Continued fraction expansions of π.

## Iterative / algorithmic
13. Gauss-Legendre algorithm (arithmetic-geometric mean), quadratically converging — used in modern high-precision computations.
14. Borwein's algorithms (various orders of convergence).

## Numerical integration
15. Integrating √(1−x²) from −1 to 1 and doubling, or integrating 4/(1+x²) from 0 to 1.

## Physical / experimental
16. Pendulum period experiments combined with g to back out π.
17. Spectral methods — e.g., counting lattice points in a circle (Gauss circle problem) to approximate π.
